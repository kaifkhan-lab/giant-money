◆ How to read this page

US law (the STOCK Act) forces every member of Congress to disclose their personal stock trades. This page collects those official filings so you can see exactly what your representatives are trading.

Trade row — who traded, which stock, buy or sell, the size range, and the date.

Insider Trades.

CEOs and boards trading their own stock — parsed from official SEC Form 4 filings the moment they're published. Clusters flagged automatically.
◆ How to read this page

When a company's own executives or directors buy or sell their company's stock, the SEC makes them file a Form 4 within two business days. This page reads those filings live — insiders know their business best, so their trades are worth watching.

Open-market buy — an insider spending their own money on their own stock, often a confidence signal.
Cluster — several insiders at the same company buying around the same time; flagged automatically.
Role — CEO, CFO, Director or 10% owner — exactly who is trading.
Sentiment — the overall balance of insider buying versus selling across the market.

Beginner FAQ

What is a 13F filing?
Every quarter, big investment firms managing over $100M must tell the SEC exactly which US stocks they hold. That report is called a 13F. It's how we know what Warren Buffett or Citadel actually own — it's public, official paperwork, and Giant Money reads it directly from SEC EDGAR.
What is a PTR?
A Periodic Transaction Report. US senators and representatives must disclose their personal stock trades, usually within 45 days. We track these official disclosures and flag when a filing came in later than the law allows.
What is a Form 4?
When a CEO, CFO, director or large owner buys or sells their own company's stock, they must report it to the SEC within 2 business days on a Form 4. Insider buying is often watched closely — executives know their business best.
Where does the data come from?
Only live public sources: SEC EDGAR (13F, Form 4, company fundamentals), official Senate and House disclosures, live market data and real financial news feeds. Nothing is simulated or made up — if a number isn't available yet, we show a dash instead of a fake value.
How fresh is it?
A real backend scheduler runs around the clock: market data every 60 seconds, insider filings every 5 minutes, news every 3 minutes, fund filings and politician disclosures several times a day — even when nobody is on the site.
Is this investment advice?
No. Giant Money is a research tool that organizes public information. It doesn't recommend buying or selling anything. Always do your own research.
