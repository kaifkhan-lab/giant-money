TOP 1% · SMART MONEY

See what the world's
richest investors own.

Any institution managing over $100 million must report its entire US stock portfolio to the SEC every quarter, on a form called the 13F-HR. That includes Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway, Ray Dalio's Bridgewater, Goldman Sachs, and the sovereign wealth funds of Norway, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Singapore and the UAE.

The filings are public — but they arrive as dense, machine-unfriendly documents that almost nobody reads. We parse every one, compare it against the previous quarter, and hand you the answer in plain English.

US CONGRESS · STOCK ACT

Watch how your
representatives trade.

The STOCK Act of 2012 makes it illegal for members of Congress to trade on non-public information — and requires every one of them to publicly disclose their personal stock trades within 45 days, on a Periodic Transaction Report.

These filings reveal something remarkable: lawmakers trading in the very industries their committees regulate, sometimes days before major news. We collect every disclosure from both the House and the Senate and make it searchable.

SEC FORM 4 · LIVE

Follow the people who
know the company best.

Company officers, directors and anyone owning more than 10% of a company are called insiders. When they buy or sell their own company's stock, US law gives them just two business days to file a Form 4 with the SEC.

This is the fastest-moving disclosure in the market — and the most revealing. An executive spending their own money on their own stock is making a statement no press release can match.

✓

Institutional holdings

Filed quarterly by every institution managing over $100 million. Lists their complete US stock portfolio — every position and its market value.

Filed when a company's officer, director or 10%+ owner buys or sells that company's stock. The fastest disclosure in US markets.

QUESTIONS

Everything you're
about to ask.

Is this real data or a simulation?

100% real. Every number comes from an official public filing — SEC EDGAR, the House Clerk, the US Senate, and live market data. We never invent a figure: if something hasn't been filed yet, you see a dash, not a guess.

Is it legal to see these trades?

Completely. US law requires this disclosure — 13F holdings reports for large institutional managers, the STOCK Act for Congress, and Section 16 Form 4s for company insiders. It is all public record. Most people simply never look.

How delayed is the information?

That depends on the law, not on us. Form 4 insider trades land within two business days. Congressional PTRs allow up to roughly 45 days. 13F fund holdings are quarterly. We surface each one the moment it's published — and always show the filing date so you can judge for yourself.

Does Giant Money cost anything?

No. It's free, and there is no real money anywhere in the product — no trading, no brokerage account, no entry fees. The Shadow Portfolio is a paper backtest only.

Is this investment advice?

No — and it never will be. Giant Money is an information platform that shows what powerful investors have publicly disclosed. What you do with that information is entirely your own decision. We are not licensed financial advisors.

Where does the data actually come from?

SEC EDGAR for 13F-HR holdings and Form 4 insider trades, the House Clerk's PTR system and the Senate eFD portal for congressional disclosures, USAspending.gov for federal contract awards, and live market feeds for prices. Every source is official and public.

Do I need to know anything about investing?

No. Every screen is written for beginners — plain-English explanations sit at the top of each page, jargon is defined inline, and the numbers are formatted so you can read them at a glance. The Glossary section above covers every filing type you'll encounter.

How often does the data update?

A backend loop runs continuously, 24 hours a day, whether or not anyone has the app open. Prices refresh throughout the trading day; filings are ingested as soon as they appear at the source.
